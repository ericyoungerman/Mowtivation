Question and experimental design

Did soybean yield differ among the five weed-management treatments, and was yield different between roller-crimped cereal rye and tilled systems? Did treatment differences vary among site-years?

The study used a split-plot randomized complete block design. Rye termination system (rolled or tilled) was randomized to whole plots within blocks, and weed-control treatments were assigned to subplots within each system. There were three rolled treatments (no supplemental control, inter-row mowing, and high-residue cultivation) and two tilled treatments (inter-row mowing and cultivation). The trial includes three site-years: 2023 Field V, 2024 O2 East, and 2024 O2 West.

The primary analysis includes all site-years. Site-year is fixed because these three specific experiments are of interest; blocks and whole plots are random. The five-level treatment factor is used because the design is not a complete system-by-weed-control factorial. Consequently, a rolled-versus-tilled contrast averages over different weed-control treatment sets and should be interpreted as a comparison of the tested management packages, not a pure termination-method effect.

Primary model

The model includes a treatment-by-site-year interaction and separate random intercepts for blocks within site-years and whole plots within blocks. The whole-plot term preserves the correct randomization level for comparisons between rolled and tilled systems. Treatment differences within a system are estimated using subplot-level information. Inspect the whole-plot variance component and singular-fit warning; do not automatically remove this term solely because its fitted variance is small.

yield_mod <- lmer(
  yield_kg_ha ~ weed_trt * site_year +
    (1 | site_year:block) + (1 | whole_plot),
  data = yield_dat, REML = TRUE
)

Estimated means and planned comparisons

Estimated marginal means give equal weight to each of the three site-years. Pairwise treatment differences and their 95% confidence intervals are reported without compact-letter displays, because a nonsignificant test does not prove the treatments are equal. Fisher’s LSD-style comparisons use emmeans with adjust = "none": all ten pairwise tests and their pointwise 95% confidence intervals are unadjusted. These exploratory comparisons do not control the family-wise error rate and are computed regardless of the omnibus treatment test.

The two targeted contrasts are separately defined, exploratory, unadjusted comparisons, each with a pointwise 95% confidence interval. They do not constitute a jointly multiplicity-adjusted family.

emm_trt <- emmeans(yield_mod, ~ weed_trt, weights = "equal")

Results summary

The primary split-plot model detected no evidence of an overall treatment effect (Satterthwaite F = 1.41; p = 0.254) or treatment-by-site-year interaction (F = 1.20; p = 0.332). Yield differed among site-years (p <0.001). The exploratory rolled-minus-tilled package difference was 46 kg/ha (pointwise 95% CI: -229 to 321; unadjusted p = 0.730). Nonsignificant difference tests do not establish equivalence.

Interpretation notes

Report the estimated contrasts, confidence intervals, and treatment-by-site-year test alongside the overall treatment test. If treatment differences are not statistically detected, conclude no evidence of a yield difference was detected, not that yields were equivalent. A formal equivalence claim would require a defensible equivalence margin chosen independently of the observed data and a correctly specified two-one-sided-test (TOST) analysis. The former Rmd’s ±10% margin based on the observed tilled mean is therefore omitted from this primary analysis.

Because site-years are fixed, conclusions describe these experiments rather than a random population of environments. Interpret results in light of the weed-biomass and stand-establishment analyses: similar yield despite differences in weed suppression can be biologically informative without demonstrating that supplemental control has no agronomic value.

get_sensitivity <- function(model, label) {
  means <- emmeans(model, ~ weed_trt, weights = "equal")

Appendix: exploratory yield noninferiority and equivalence

The primary scientific question here is whether the rolled management packages avoid an agronomically important yield penalty relative to the tilled packages. We therefore emphasize noninferiority (rolled minus tilled yield greater than −450 kg/ha), followed by a two-sided equivalence check (the difference within ±450 kg/ha). These are different hypotheses, and neither follows from a nonsignificant conventional treatment test.

The fixed working margin of 450 kg/ha is approximately 10% of a rounded 4,500 kg/ha soybean yield benchmark (approximately 66.9 bu/ac). The 450 kg/ha margin is approximately 6.7 bu/ac. This benchmark is a transparent working assumption, not an independently validated economic threshold. It must be justified using agronomic or economic considerations before confirmatory manuscript claims. These analyses are exploratory because the margin and hypotheses were revisited after seeing the yield results. The margin is intentionally not recalculated from the observed TIC or rolled/tilled model means. Margins of 225 and 675 kg/ha (approximately 5% and 15% of the same benchmark) illustrate sensitivity to the chosen threshold.

The analysis uses the same split-plot model, equal weighting of site-years, and equal weighting of the three rolled and two tilled management packages. Because these packages contain different supplemental weed-control treatments, the contrast is not an isolated effect of termination method.
